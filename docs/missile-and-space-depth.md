# Missile identity and space depth

The radial star motion and missile sizes described in this original pass are superseded by [forward travel refinement](forward-travel-refinement.md).

Rockets and homing missiles now use separate mechanical silhouettes on a shared 512×512 two-row WebP atlas. Rockets are dark/red with amber markings and orange exhaust; homing weapons are silver/cyan with blue exhaust. The missile material rectangles are 86×48 and 78×44 before viewport scaling, with normal blending to preserve metal detail. The atlas was normalized using alpha bounds above 24 plus an 8px fringe and a common scale, avoiding distant near-transparent pixels shrinking the weapons. Each frame retains transparent filtering gutters. It adds one shared texture source (approximately 1.33 MiB including mipmaps), not a source per missile.

A single cyan bracket reticle marks an actual acquired homing target. Target identity is collected during existing missile rendering loops and resolved against live enemies. No marker appears without a target ID; predicted guest shots without an authoritative target ID do not invent a lock.

Player rockets launch at 82% of their previous velocity (the common -72 to -76 vertical launch becomes approximately -59 to -62). Homing launch velocity is 90%, and guided speed is bounded to 52–66 field units/second instead of a minimum of 62 without an upper cap. Lasers and other fast weapons retain their velocities. Damage, fire cadence, turn-rate settings and collision radii are unchanged; travel time is intentionally different.

The starfield now divides the existing quality-dependent unique-star budget between cached distant points and pooled perspective-projected foreground quads. Low uses 28 distant points and 12 short depth points; medium/high/ultra use 45/72/99 projected points respectively. Projection uses depth-dependent size, brightness, perspective expansion, steering parallax and edge/wrap fades. One reusable output record avoids per-star allocation. Old flat near-star geometry is disabled. Water and volcanic arenas retain their planetary scenery instead of space stars.

Planet layers use separate depth factors for apparent size, brightness, scrolling speed and steering response. Distant planets move more slowly and dimmer; nearby planets move more strongly. The old whole-image rotation is reduced to avoid a spinning-flat-picture appearance. These remain shaded sprite planets with depth cues, not newly rendered 3D sphere meshes. Quality-dependent planet visibility limits remain in effect.

Validation: production build and packaging; missile material identity/size checks; speed-policy bounds; perspective scale/brightness, finite coordinates and wrap fades; existing boss-art and travel tests. In-game appearance and device FPS remain unverified. CPU transforms for the bounded perspective stars replace the previous flat-layer treatment, so this is not a claim of zero performance cost.
